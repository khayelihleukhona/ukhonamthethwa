Painting, sculpture, sound and installation Umphako Wendlela, 2025

About

Painting is my primary medium, but my practice has expanded into sculpture, sound and installation, using rust and indigenous grass-mat making structures.

Ukhona Mthethwa (b. 2001) is a visual artist from Dannhauser, a small mining town in KwaZulu-Natal. He lives and works in Pretoria, South Africa. He is completing a Master's degree in Fine and Studio Arts at Tshwane University of Technology (TUT), where he earned a Postgraduate Diploma in Fine and Applied Arts, cum laude.

His work uses a dark colour palette and symbolism that reflects both personal and collective experiences. He has shown in group exhibitions at The Viewing Room Art Gallery, Carnegie Art Gallery and Trent Gallery, and at Capital Art Fair Tshwane. In 2026 he was a finalist for Sasol New Signatures and a top five finalist for the Cassirer Welz Award.

Practice

My work explores memory, lineage and transformation through ancestral archetypes, mythology and African folktales.

Umphako Wendlela is a Zulu phrase meaning “provision for a journey”. It answers to what I carried from home: the stories told on my family’s grass mat, the history of the Dingiswayo lineage, and the sound and vibration of the mines around my community in Dannhauser.

My father is a steelworker. When the mines blast, the storage space where he keeps his metalwork shakes. Away from home, that sound returns to me as memory, and it runs through the paintings, the structures and the sound in this work.

Painting

Rust and acrylic on canvas. Through abstraction and the layering of figures, the paintings reflect on the shadows of history that follow us and the realities of the present.

Rust

Rust is a preservative of time. It connects the work to my father’s world of steel and to the broader history of mining around my hometown.

Grass-mat structures

The grass-mat making structure is both material and metaphor: a vessel of memory that holds the woven stories of my ancestry. Canvases hang from wooden frames and spill onto the floor.

Sound and installation

Sound echoes the explosions of the mines and the clattering of my father’s metalwork. In Ayikho Indlu (“there is no home”), small framed canvases rest on red bricks with steel bars, wire and an enamel cup.
